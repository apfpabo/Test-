# Where Iron Meridian stands (12 Nov 2026)

A comparison with the games it is closest to, area by area. Iron Meridian is a captain's sandbox (Starsector's shape)
on an alternate Earth's oceans, fought with ships modelled on real ones from 1944 to the present (Rule the Waves'
material). No other game puts those two together; each area below is measured against the game that does it best.

The games: **Rule the Waves 3** (Matrix/NWS; Expanded Battles expansion and refit updates, spring 2026), **Starsector**
(Fractal Softworks; 0.98a, 2025), **Ultimate Admiral: Dreadnoughts** (Game-Labs), **Sea Power: Naval Combat in the
Missile Age** (Triassic Games; early access, 0.8.0 in July 2026, aiming for 1.0 late in 2026), **Cold Waters** (Killerfish),
**NEBULOUS: Fleet Command** (Eridanus Industries) and **Highfleet** (Konstantin Koshutin). Facts about other games are from
their own pages and from memory up to mid-2026; where a detail could be out of date it is put loosely.

## The short answer

- **Ahead or level:** the span of eras and ship types in one game (gun battleships to stealth destroyers, carriers,
  submarines and missiles under one model); the strategic sandbox (economy, diplomacy, contracts, your own flag and ports,
  land structures, nuclear weapons); sensors and emissions in battle; the look (lit ship plans, effects, weather, the globe).
- **Behind:** balance (almost every number is untuned); depth of the damage and armour model (Rule the Waves);
  depth of the ship designer (Rule the Waves, Ultimate Admiral); hand-written story and content (Starsector); modern
  sensor and missile depth (Sea Power, NEBULOUS); and the things a released game has: save slots, key rebinding,
  difficulty settings, mod support, translations.
- **Biggest single gap:** tuning. The systems are mostly there; how they play together over a campaign has been
  measured (the tuning sheet) but not yet decided.

## What would close the gaps (in the agent's order, for the owner to choose)

1. **Tuning** ([`TUNING_SHEET.md`](TUNING_SHEET.md)): the first months' money, the hunters, prices, and the numbers
   marked untuned. This decides whether the game is fun more than anything below.
2. **Release basics:** several save slots, a difficulty setting (money, hunters, damage), key rebinding.
3. **Content:** more story chains and hand-made encounters per power; a main story arc of the owner's lore.
4. **Officers:** skill growth and a few traits per officer, so a captain is worth keeping.
5. **Armour by place** (belt, deck, turrets, angle of fall): the owner has left this open; it would bring the damage
   model level with Rule the Waves.
6. **Sound and music:** more pieces and battle ambience.
7. **Mod support:** ships, weapons and factions already live in data files; loading extra ones from a folder is a short
   step.
8. **Translations,** once the text settles.

Decisions waiting for the owner are kept in [`OWNER_DECISIONS.md`](OWNER_DECISIONS.md).
