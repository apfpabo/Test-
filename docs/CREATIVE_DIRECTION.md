### Core Vision / Elevator Pitch
You are a free captain in a fractured Alternate Earth still scarred by the Great War of the 1940s. Starting with a single outdated destroyer or freighter, you build a personal fleet through combat, trade, salvage, and exploration across the world’s oceans. You can serve existing powers, carve out your own maritime domain, or remain a pure independent. Technology advances only through what you (and the world) discover and research — from late-WWII gunnery and early radar to Cold War missiles, nuclear propulsion, and modern networked warfare.

The tone stays grounded, logistical, and unforgiving: fuel, ammunition, crew fatigue, weather, and supply lines matter. Glory is possible, but so is a quiet death in a forgotten sea.

### Setting Frame – Alternate Earth
**Point of Divergence (suggested):** The Second World War ends in a messy, incomplete armistice rather than total victory. Nuclear weapons are delayed or suppressed by early treaties and sabotage. Air power never fully eclipses surface fleets the way it did in our timeline, so capital ships, carriers, and later missile platforms remain decisive. Continental powers are weaker or more fragmented; sea control is the real currency of empire.

The world is global and interconnected by ocean. Major theaters:
- North Atlantic & Arctic routes
- Mediterranean & Black Sea choke points
- Indian Ocean trade arteries
- Pacific island chains and resource archipelagos
- Southern Ocean / Antarctic approaches (dangerous, high-reward)

Ports replace colonies. Control of a major port gives shipyards, markets, recruitment, and political leverage. Abandoned wartime bases, scuttled fleets, and isolated research stations serve as the “derelict” and “domain ruins” equivalents.

### Progression & Technology
Start firmly in late-WWII / early postwar tech (roughly 1944–1952 equivalent). Research points are earned by:
- Salvaging advanced wrecks
- Capturing enemy officers and technical documents
- Completing exploration objectives and surveying remote sites
- Trading with or raiding specialized research enclaves
- Faction reputation rewards and story missions

Research unlocks successive “eras” (or partial trees within eras):
1. **Late War / Immediate Postwar** – heavy guns, early radar, piston aircraft, primitive missiles, conventional submarines
2. **Early Cold War** – jet aircraft, guided missiles, better sonar, nuclear propulsion prototypes, early SAMs
3. **Late Cold War** – mature missile doctrine, nuclear attack submarines, advanced electronics, vertical launch systems
4. **Modern** – network-centric warfare, stealth features, long-range precision strike, unmanned systems (limited)

You don’t unlock everything at once. Different factions and regions progress at different rates, creating natural technological disparity and scavenging opportunities.

### Ship Classes & Combat Feel
Keep Starsector’s satisfying fleet combat but ground it in naval reality:

- **Escorts**: Corvettes, frigates, destroyers (screening, ASW, anti-air)
- **Cruisers**: Heavy and light — the workhorses for independent operations
- **Capital ships**: Battleships (gun-line monsters early on), battlecruisers, heavy missile cruisers later
- **Carriers**: From escort carriers to fleet carriers; aircraft are a major force multiplier and logistical burden
- **Submarines**: Stealth, ambush, and sea denial (strong early, evolve into nuclear boats)
- **Auxiliaries**: Tankers, ammunition ships, repair ships, troop transports — logistics is half the game

Combat emphasizes range bands, ammunition types, weather, time of day, radar/sonar horizons, and formation discipline. Fuel and magazine depth matter. A well-handled older fleet can still savage a careless modern one if it controls the engagement geometry.

### Factions (starting point suggestions)
- Legacy empires and successor states still fighting over the unfinished war
- Rising industrial powers with strong merchant marines and ambitious navies
- Corporate consortiums that field private fleets to protect trade
- Revolutionary or breakaway regimes
- Pirate syndicates and freeport alliances
- Isolated research or “neutral” scientific enclaves that hold advanced tech

You can join, betray, or found your own flag. Creating your own faction means claiming ports, attracting officers and ships, managing reputation with the great powers, and eventually becoming a player on the global stage.

### Campaign Structure
Open-world global ocean map with:
- Dynamic trade routes and convoy systems
- Seasonal weather and ice
- Procedural and hand-crafted points of interest (wreck fields, black sites, contested islands, smuggling dens)
- Reputation and story arcs that react to your growing power and technological era
- The long-term possibility of tipping the balance of the unfinished war or forging a new maritime order

### What Makes It Distinct
- True multi-era progression felt in the hulls you sail and the weapons you face
- Logistics and sea control as first-class gameplay (not just background)
- The emotional weight of captaining ships that feel like real steel and history, even in an alternate world
- The classic Starsector power fantasy of going from one battered ship to a personal armada that can challenge nations — now measured in displacement tons and battle flags instead of flux capacitors
