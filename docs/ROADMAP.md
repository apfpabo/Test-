# Roadmap

How the current build lines up with [`CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md`](CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md) (the north star), plus standing art and design notes from playtest feedback. Update this file as features land.

## Standing design notes (owner feedback)

- **Grounded, not arcade.** Map fleets need momentum: they accelerate and turn gradually, and the bow must not snap to a new heading. Combat pace should feel like steel ships, not fighters.
- **Art reference: Rule the Waves 3.** Realistic top-down ship plans at true proportions, with one consistent paint and lighting scheme across hull, superstructure and turrets. No cartoon outlines or bright decorative colours. Wakes on both the map and in battle.
- **Ship variety by era and manufacturer** (first pass done), as in Starsector. Several yards each field their own design per class, modelled on real ships (Iowa, Yamato, Bismarck, KGV, Fletcher and so on) under fictional names.
- **No boarding.** Capturing ships that strike their colours is fine.
- **Turrets and superstructures (art pass 2, done 10 Nov 2026):** after feedback that adjacent turrets blended together, turrets lacked national character and superstructures were low-detail. Turrets and superstructures are now baked with height maps that Godot lights from the sun's direction, with shadows cast between levels. Gun mounts follow the shipbuilder's national style, and superstructures are built up by national school. Brief and notes: [`NEXT_ART_PASS.md`](NEXT_ART_PASS.md).
- **Playtest feedback, 10 Nov 2026 (done):** map zoom lag fixed; refit is planned and confirmed (undo, standard fit, weapon cards with stats); punchier gun sound and visuals, light AA no longer silent, engine and sea sound; wakes for every ship on the chart; damageable subsystems (magazines, machinery, steering, fire control, crew).
- **Playtest feedback, round 2 (done):**
  - "Battle stations" replaces "Action stations"; the aiming readout says "turning to bear" and "blocked by own ship".
  - The sea moves: travelling swell on the real clock, glints that ride it, cloud shadows.
  - Muzzle blasts spread outboard only, not back across the ship.
  - Guns fire barrel by barrel.
  - Gun reports have more bass, and crowd control keeps big fights distinct.
  - Ship engines and the sea along the hull are now audible in battle and on the chart.
  - Ships in a column follow the leader's track, so they stay off the land.
  - A damage schematic of the flagship shows mounts, magazines, machinery, steering and fire control.
  - The owner is sending a reference picture for the damage display; match it when it arrives.
- **Playtest feedback, round 3 (done):**
  - Pause menu on Esc: resume; restart or set up the battle again (quick battles); break off or quit (campaign).
  - Finite ammunition: shells per gun by calibre, AA bursts, one torpedo reload, missile salvos, SAM rounds, depth-charge patterns. Campaign ships keep what they have left; rearm in port.
  - Submarines: homing torpedoes can attack submerged boats; boats hear each other on passive sonar; escorts with sonar run in over a submerged contact to depth-charge it; Z drops depth charges by hand.
  - Missile launches show flame, a white cloud and dark smoke, and the booster burns for the first second and a half.
  - CIWS, Oerlikon and Bofors each have their own rate-of-fire sound.
  - The damage display follows War Thunder's: damage-only silhouette plus a badge row (crew, fires, machinery, ammunition, steering, fire control).
  - Recorded sound effects (owner approved CC0 and CC BY) replace the synthesised ones: gun reports by calibre, hits, splashes on misses, blasts, missile launches, depth charges, sea and engine-room loops; CIWS and light AA are built from recorded transients at each weapon's rate of fire. Sources: `godot/assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`.
- **Playtest feedback, round 4 (done):**
  - Settings (title screen and pause menu): master, effects and ambience volume; battle minimap small, medium or large.
  - Sound:
    - Chart engine sound is quieter.
    - Ship sounds fade instead of stopping dead.
    - Earshot in battle is measured in metres, so zooming in no longer cuts sounds off.
    - A sea bed and gulls play on the chart (more gulls near land) and at sea in battle.
  - Barrels in a salvo fire further apart.
  - Struck ships cease fire.
  - The flagship panel shows depth-charge patterns and rack reloading.
  - Hovering the damage display explains each badge and the damage colours.
  - Battle islands are shaded terrain (beach, scrub, woods, rock, peaks, shallows and surf), with the coastline kept to the collision outline.
  - Shell ballistics:
    - Each shell's penetration depends on its weight and range.
    - It meets belt armour made thicker by the angle of impact, or the thinner deck when it plunges.
    - Results: ricochet, no penetration (splinters), over-penetration, or penetration (full damage, subsystems).
    - Markers name the result on hits by and on your ships.
- **Starting origins (done):** the eight origins the owner wrote ([`LORE.md`](LORE.md)) replace the three old starts. Each has its own ships, money, standing, captain and lasting conditions (limited services at an old flag, hunter groups, trade, evasion and prize perks, intelligence as research points). Navy origins let the player choose the navy. LORE.md lists what the origins promise but the game doesn't do yet.
- **Playtest feedback, round 5 (done, 9 Nov 2026):**
  - A fictional world replaces the real Earth (the owner chose candidate C of four). Old saves are refused (save version 4) with a note on the title screen.
  - Regional faction names follow the new map (owner's decision): Hesperian Concord, Vargan Directorate, Halcyon Federation, Tarsene Free Republic. The yards that named real places are renamed too (Calder & Wexmouth, Strelsk-Werke, Korvagrad Admiralty, Amakura Arsenal, Cantieri del Corvo).
  - Realistic engagement radius on the chart: contact at each side's reach instead of on collision. Guns about 90 km, anti-ship missiles about 120 km, escort carriers 250 km, fleet carriers 350 km. Your reach and hostile missile or carrier groups' reach show as dashed rings; the contact panel says at what range and whose ships found whom. A force that has been met stays quiet until the range opens past its reach again. Follow-up: battles still open at the usual distance, so a missile or carrier contact doesn't yet start the battle at long range.
  - The chart's land is shaded terrain in the battle islands' palette (owner's question: could the islands' look be used on the world map?), with climate by latitude and fine detail when zoomed in.
- **Foundational lore (done, 9 Nov 2026):** the owner's lore for the new world is in the game: a codex (title screen and Command panel), 1946 backstories on the origin screens, faction descriptions and attitudes, the mysteries as survey sites and port rumours, raider grounds and garrisons from the March 1946 situation. [`LORE.md`](LORE.md) lists where lore and game still differ (the armistice above all).
- **Playtest feedback, round 6 (done, 9 Nov 2026):**
  - Chart movement: fleets now move the way their bows point and steer onto the route through a turning circle, so they no longer slide sideways while the bow swings. A hard turn costs about a third of the speed instead of nearly all of it. `tests/move_test.tscn` checks slip, speed in a turn and a long passage without grounding.
  - Contact: any hostile force inside gun range makes contact, even one met earlier (a chasing raider could sit alongside without a fight).
  - Esc on the chart opens a pause menu: resume, Command, Codex, settings, save and exit to title, save and quit.
  - The Sound button mutes everything (sea and gulls included) and says whether sound is on.
  - Chart sound is quieter: the sea bed at about half, engines at half.
  - The Command panel labels the hull bar ("Hull 70%"), with a tooltip, and the bar no longer stretches into a dark box.
  - Pack ice reads as ice: a broken white edge, loose floes off it, and a "PACK ICE · MAR LIMIT" label that follows the season.
  - Gunnery: director-style salvos. Turrets fire together, rippling fore to aft a fraction of a second apart, in alternating half-salvos (one gun of each turret, then the others half a reload later), as 1940s battleships fired while spotting the fall of shot.
  - The world follows the owner's lore: see [`LORE.md`](LORE.md) ("Changed to follow the lore").
  - The armistice (owner approved): warships engage you on sight only at standing -60, after an incident (you opened fire on that flag: three months), or as an origin's declared target. Ports close below -10. The standing list says which flags are at war with you, which ports are closed, and for how long an incident lasts.
  - Question answered: the lines that stop short of the range ring in battle are shells still in flight (the white dots at their heads); the ring is the guns' maximum range.
- **Effects overhaul (done, 9 Nov 2026):** the owner found the effects reminiscent of old Flash games and asked for something livelier, like Starsector's. Modern effects (Settings, "Battle effects: Modern / Classic"; classic is the earlier look, kept as a fallback):
  - Explosions in layers: a white-hot core, rolling fireballs cooling from yellow to red, a brief shockwave ring, debris streaking out, embers drifting downwind, and a pool of light on the sea and the hulls.
  - Shell splashes: a burst with spray clouds thrown out, a ring across the water, lingering foam; heavy shells leave more.
  - Smoke shaded by the sun and turning as it rises; fresh smoke over a fire glows warm.
  - Ship fires flicker and light the deck and water around them; shells fly as glowing streaks; missiles show a flame and glow.
  - Wakes: bow spray flung out at speed, a churning stern, and sun glints twinkling in the wake.
  - A magazine explosion lights the whole sky for a moment.
  - Backup: the commit before the overhaul is `3dbf780` (local tag `backup-before-fx`), and the owner has that build (IronMeridian_backup_before_fx.zip). `tests/shot_fx.tscn -- <dir> <modern|classic>` captures both styles for comparison.
- **Playtest feedback, round 7 (done, 9 Nov 2026):**
  - Refit: the mount being changed is ringed in gold on the ship's plan; pressing Details far down the list keeps the scroll position (every port and Command panel now keeps its scroll when it redraws itself).
  - Ship systems, fitted in port (Refit tab) in two marks each: machinery (+4% speed a mark, battle and chart), hull and armour (+8% hull, +10% armour), magazine protection (fewer magazine fires, more time to flood them), fire control (salvos 12% tighter), radar (+6% gun range), sonar (+30% range, sonar ships only). Mk II radar and sonar need the research. Costs are a share of the hull's price.
  - An orders box at the top right of the chart lists your story orders and contracts with days left; hover for the full brief (who posted it, pay, deadline, where the target is and how far), click to plot a course to the target. Contracts in Command show the same brief on hover.
  - The battle's flagship panel shows each battery's reload: the bar fills as the guns reload (gold), turns green when ready, and the seconds left are shown.
  - Dry bunkers now carry into battle: your ships make only steerage way (a quarter of their speed), and the battle says so.
- **Land structures (done, 9 Nov 2026):** step 4 of the owner's plan, with the owner's answers to the brief ([`LAND_STRUCTURES.md`](LAND_STRUCTURES.md)):
  - 384 structures: farms, steelworks, oil and industrial works on the inland sites, and 118 military installations (coastal batteries by the ports and on both shores of every strait, airfields, radar stations, 16 old naval bases), each held by the flag of its port, or unclaimed in the far south.
  - Air strikes from your carriers, auto-resolved (fighters with air cover from nearby airfields, flak, bombs against how hard the target is), shown as a short watchable raid with a skip button, then a report. Aircraft lost in strikes are replaced in port. A strike on a flag you are at peace with breaks the armistice.
  - Capture by surrender once 80% of the defences are silenced and your fleet lies off the coast: a garrison lands from your crews (`Structures.landing_force()` is where troop transports will go). Unclaimed sites take only a garrison.
  - Monthly income: money paid, food and steel to a port's Warehouse tab (or your naval base, or the site itself). Holdings tab in Command.
  - Airfields spot you and vector patrols onto you; coastal batteries shell your ships within 35 km; radar strengthens defences and warns you of assaults; naval bases you hold serve as a dockyard.
  - The powers take each other's structures in wars, structures follow their port when it falls, and the flag you took one from sends assault forces with troopships (shown in the orders box) to retake it. Strike contracts on the boards of powers at war.
  - Map symbols at normal zoom, drawn installations (runways, casemates, tank farms, pitheads, fields, piers) from 3× zoom.
- **Right-click menu on the chart (done, 9 Nov 2026, owner request):** right-click a fleet: friendly ones offer *Escort her* (keep station astern at her speed, no contact; handy for escort contracts), *Close and hail* (the signals contact) and *Cancel orders*; hostile ones *Intercept*. The escorted fleet is ringed in green. `tests/escort_test.tscn`, `shot_context.tscn`.
- **Owner follow-ups (done, 9 Nov 2026):** the raid view now uses the battle's own effects (modern or classic, as set in Settings): layered explosions with fireballs, shock rings, debris and embers, shaded smoke, burning fires and flak. Freeport yards sell the Syndicate's improvised carriers (second-hand escort carriers). The chart's "triangulation failed" errors are fixed: small ice floes at large map coordinates were too fine for the triangulator, so each floe is now drawn round its own centre.
- **Playtest feedback, round 8 (done, 9 Nov 2026):**
  - Ship and wake on the chart at deep zoom: ships are drawn larger than life, so close in a ship was longer than its own turning circle and seemed to spin on the spot, its wake arms crossing into loops. Close in, a ship is now kept within 1.8 turning radii, the ring and label hug it, and the wake arms never spread wider than the turn (`tests/shot_turn.tscn`).
  - Torpedoes run under the water: a dark shape and a pale body that comes and goes with the swell (warm-tinted for the enemy's), drawn under the ships, and a fainter bubble track (`tests/shot_torps.tscn`).
  - The top bar shows the flag you serve (commission or letter of marque, in its colour, with the details on hover) or "Independent".
  - Ctrl+Click adds a waypoint: on the chart a further leg from the end of the course, through the sea lanes, marked with a ring; in battle the flagship steers through its waypoints by itself (A/D takes the helm back, Ctrl+right-click clears) (`tests/waypoint_test.tscn`).
  - Structures within reach of a fleet's guns or missiles can be bombarded from the sea ([`LAND_STRUCTURES.md`](LAND_STRUCTURES.md)).
  - A more modern interface: Barlow and Barlow Condensed (SIL Open Font Licence, `godot/assets/fonts/OFL.txt`) for all text, near-black flat panels with hairline borders and a gold rule, condensed capital titles, underlined tabs, flat buttons that light on hover, section headers with rules, styled tooltips, popups and slim scrollbars, two-line stat chips and a segmented time control in the top bar, a hero title on the title screen. `UI.setup` puts it on every screen.
  - Windows SmartScreen warns about the .exe because it is not code-signed (see the note to the owner); "More info" then "Run anyway" starts it.
- **Playtest feedback, round 9 (done, 9 Nov 2026):**
  - Clicking the chart locked on a fleet far from the cursor: the hit radius was in map units and grew huge close in. Fleets, ports, sites and structures are now picked within a few screen pixels (or a fleet's drawn size), the nearest first.
  - Land structures in scale: batteries, radar stations and naval bases stand on the coast itself (2 km inland; the world grid had put them 11 km in); inland structures are drawn close in no larger than their distance from the sea; "Set course for the coast" aims at a reachable point a little offshore, so the fleet no longer circles near the shore (`tests/shot_coast.tscn`).
  - Waypoints closer together than the turning circle (or a slow ship) made the fleet orbit one for ever: a point inside the turning circle and well off the bow now counts as passed (`waypoint_test` checks a close zig-zag on dry bunkers).
  - The "white box" at a ship's stern was the white flag of a ship that had struck her colours, drawn as a plain rectangle: now a small white flag on a staff at the stern, streaming astern in the wind over the deck and rippling, with its shadow.
  - Barrel recoil (already modelled per barrel) is heavier and slower: the barrel slams back about a fifth of its length and runs out over two seconds.
  - The battle's status panel mirrors the flagship: her plan in her paint, every turret trained to its real bearing, shell holes and soot, knocked-out mounts crossed out, machinery and steering damage, flooded magazines; no fire markers (fires are counted in the badge row) (`tests/shot_mirror.tscn`).
  - AP, HE and incendiary shells: auto by target (AP against armour it can beat, HE against light ships or armour it can't, incendiary against merchants) or chosen with V for the flagship. HE bursts on contact: little penetration, no over-penetration, more blast against unarmoured ships, twice the fires; incendiary four times the fires. One magazine per mount. Hit markers name the shell.
  - Fuel: the reserve is counted (about four days at a crawl, burned at a third of the rate, filled again from the bunkers); when it is gone the squadron is dead in the water. Right-click a friendly fleet to call it by wireless (about 5,000 km): it comes to you. A warship offers a tow to the nearest open port for a fee; any merchant convoy offers Trade, a small market at sea from its holds (fuel always among them) with its own cash and prices a third worse than a port's. "Hail her now" when she is within signalling distance (`tests/fuel_test.tscn`).
  - Crew on the chart: a squadron short-handed steams slower (35% of speed with nobody in the stokeholds, rising with the crew), and with no crew at all she is adrift and cannot raise steam. A captain who cannot pay for a tow is towed anyway (standing -5 with that flag).
- **Engine-room telegraph on the chart (done, owner request):** Slow, Half, Cruise, Full and Flank (bottom right, or Q and E). Speed is a share of cruising speed (50%, 75%, 100%, 120%, 138% = top speed) and fuel a day rises steeply with it (35%, 60%, 100%, 160%, 240%), so slow steaming stretches the bunkers and flank speed drains them. The panel shows knots and fuel a day; the top bar's days of fuel and the arrival estimate follow the setting.
- **Interface round 10 (done, owner request):**
  - Title screen centred (it had sat in the top-left corner on a window larger than the design size: the menu set its anchors but not its offsets), with a home page (Continue, New campaign, Quick battle, The world, Settings) and a New campaign page for the origins.
  - The title in iron: brushed-steel capitals with rust and pitting (`shaders/iron_title.gdshader`), a drop shadow and a riveted rule.
  - Test mode (a checkbox on the New campaign page): every technology researched, every design in every shipyard, money and research points topped up every day (`Campaign.test_mode()`); the date says TEST.
  - The chart: the Command tabs sit at the bottom left (they open Command at that page, or close it), above the panel layer so they stay clickable; toasts moved up above them; the Command button is gone; the Sound button moved to the pause menu; the time controls and Centre stand alone on the right.
- **Each power's own Quiet Race (done, owner request):** enemy fleets no longer follow your era. Every power has its own research points, growing daily at its own pace (Combine 1.2, Directorate 1.15, Concord 1.0, Federation 0.95, Enclaves 0.9, Republic 0.65, Ombria and the Isles 0.6, freeports 0.55; 30% faster at war); 400 points for Era II, 800 for Era III (`Campaign.fac_era`, `RESEARCH_PACE`). Its ships, the guns and air of its fleets in battle, and its structures' flak follow its own era; raiders run a step behind the freeports. Intelligence reports when a power reaches a new era, and the Research tab lists every power's era.
- **Transitions (done, owner request):** pages no longer snap. The title screen keeps its title in place and cross-fades its pages (the panel fades out, changes, and fades back in as it settles: `main_menu.go`); chart panels fade and scale in when they open, fade out when they close, and fade in a new page or tab, while a redraw of the same page (a button press) stays instant (`world_map.open_panel`). Command pages are titled by their tab alone, and the Command panel's "Save & exit to title" went (it is in the pause menu).
- **Ship pictures in panels:** a ship's plan is drawn as large as fits inside its box (`ShipThumb.px_per_m`), so a box stretched wider by its panel no longer makes the ship spill over the title rule and text (contact, yard, refit); small craft get a picture at least 260 px wide (`tests/shot_contact.tscn`).
- **Volume (owner request):** everything plays through a fixed output level of 10% (`Settings.OUTPUT`, Master bus at -20 dB), so 100% on the sliders is what 10% used to be. Settings saved on the old scale are carried over at the same loudness (effects and ambience times ten, `scale=2` in settings.cfg).
- **Ship poster:** `tests/shot_designs.tscn` renders every design to one scale, grouped by class (`docs/ship_designs.jpg`).
- **Ships, troopships and research round (done, 9 Nov 2026, owner request):**
  - Research: eras are explicit on the Research screen (an era opens once three technologies of the one before are researched; progress and "Opens with Era X" are shown), and there are 17 technologies in three eras instead of 8, each with an effect in battle or a class it unlocks.
  - Troop transports and landings: four troopship designs, soldiers embarked in port, "Land the troops" on a structure's panel with the odds beforehand and a report after ([`LAND_STRUCTURES.md`](LAND_STRUCTURES.md)).
  - Wartime carriers: fleet carriers no longer need jets. Shokaku and Essex are Era I; Ryujo is a flat-top with no island (the bridge under the forward lip of the deck). Jet carriers need Jet aircraft, the nuclear carrier Advanced naval reactors.
  - New designs (76 in all, from 50): Nelson, Scharnhorst, Shokaku, Ryujo, Essex, Fubuki, Atlanta; Foxtrot, Tang, Charlie; Arleigh Burke, Sovremenny, Los Angeles, Akula, Oscar, Typhoon, Nimitz, Kirov; and eight auxiliaries. All under fictional class names.
  - Battleships re-laid from recognition plans: Iowa from the owner's reference (turrets at +56, +34 and -65 m, superstructure from +24 to -54 m, two funnels, fore and aft directors), then KGV, Bismarck, Yamato, Littorio and Vanguard. Each now has the long deckhouse its bridge, funnels and after control stand on. Nelson has her three turrets forward (the third on the deck behind the raised second, so it cannot fire ahead) and a tower bridge.
  - Plan detail: the waist of every battleship is built up (an upper deckhouse, funnel casings with searchlights, AA tubs and ready-use lockers along the edges, skylights, ventilators, boats and a crane: `houseDetail`), after the owner found it bare; plating seams and edge lighting on every superstructure level, helicopter decks, tanker piping and catwalks, missile-tube hatches on the big submarines, catapult tracks, tie-downs, crash barriers and gun galleries on flight decks.
- **Three systems from the Starsector comparison (done, owner approved):**
  - Auxiliaries that work at sea. A fleet oiler carries fuel beyond the bunkers (bunkers now hold forty days' steaming, plus the oilers' tanks; fuel could be bought without limit before). An ammunition ship rearms the squadron at sea, 40 tons of munitions a day from the holds. A repair ship patches hulls at sea up to 75%, 15 tons of steel a day from the holds.
  - Captured designs. The first prize of each design is measured and her drawings copied: research points at once (double for an era you have not reached), and a naval base you hold can build the class from then on, whatever its era.
  - Battles open at contact range. A contact made at missile or carrier reach opens the action up to 18 km apart instead of 9, so missiles and aircraft strike before the guns can answer.
  - Done since from that comparison: weather and night, squadron orders and choosing which ships to commit, readiness. Also done: running dark on the chart, salvage after battle. Still open: a supply-based fleet limit instead of the cap of 10; an economy that reacts to wars and sieges.
- **Effects, second pass (done, owner request, Starsector as the reference for richness):**
  - Bloom: bright light (flashes, fires, tracers, sun on spray) bleeds into what is around it (`shaders/bloom.gdshader`, modern effects only).
  - Wakes in battle are ribbons of churned water, mottled and feathered at the edges, with the bow waves opening astern, instead of chains of dots.
  - Big explosions have a streak of glare, a black heart of oily smoke lit red from inside, burning fragments that trail smoke, and late secondary bursts.
- **Your own flag, first part (done, 9 Nov 2026; step 5 of the owner's plan):** found a flag once you hold a naval base or three structures, storm ports with troops, hold them with a garrison, collect harbour dues, use their yards, and fight off the old owner's relief forces ([`OWN_FLAG.md`](OWN_FLAG.md)).
- **Playtest feedback, round 13 (done, 10 Nov 2026):**
  - Fleets on the chart are hidden unless somebody of yours has them in sight: your squadron (about 550 km by day, less in weather and darkness, farther with a carrier's search aircraft), your main body and guard squadrons, the fleets of the flag you serve and its coalition, and the ports and structures you hold (radar stations and airfields farthest). A fleet that passes out of sight stays for five days as a grey mark where it was last seen, with how long ago. Hidden fleets cannot be clicked, and an intercept is dropped when its contact is lost. Your own and allied fleets, a convoy you escort, a fleet answering your call and an assault your radar has reported are always shown.
  - Because distant friends are no longer on the chart to click, right-clicking open sea offers a wireless call to the nearest friendly warship or merchant convoy.
  - A red "Send a distress call" button appears when you are dead in the water (owner request): the nearest warship that is not your enemy, friendly or merely neutral, comes to you and offers a tow. The fee grows with the distance and depends on your standing with her flag: half at standing 60, the plain price at 20, double at -60.
  - Sea routes: a big squadron swings wide, could fetch up hard against a coast (the Strelsk Sounds), and from there no lane point was in clear sight, so every course was refused as "no sea route". A course from such a place now stands off to open water first. `tests/sight_test` tries 365 such places round every port.
- **Salvage and running dark (done, 10 Nov 2026):**
  - Salvage after a victory: steel, munitions and fuel oil from each ship sunk, money for fittings, and research from a wreck of an era ahead of yours. Shoal water (land within about 150 km) adds half as much again; a salvage ship in company (a new design, the Grappler class, sold by Combine yards) doubles it. What the holds cannot take is left. The aftermath screen has a Salvage section.
  - Running dark on the chart: how easily you are found now depends on what you do. Speed makes smoke and wake (Slow 70% of the usual distance, Flank 130%). The Run dark button under the engine telegraph darkens ship and silences the wireless: 60% by night, 85% by day, engines held to Half or below, and no wireless calls. A wireless call gives your position away for a day (150%). It applies to patrols, hunters and search planes, on top of the weather and their radar.
- **Weather and night (done, 10 Nov 2026, owner request):** sea state, fog, daylight and the moon from the place and the calendar; on the chart they slow ships, cost fuel, batter small ships in storms, ground aircraft and shorten the distance at which forces find each other (radar helps); a battle is fought in the weather and light of the place, with ships beyond sight unseen until they fire, burn or come within radar range ([`WEATHER.md`](WEATHER.md)).
- **Squadron orders, reserve, divisions and readiness (done, 10 Nov 2026):**
  - Battle orders in a row at the top left: 1 Engage, 2 Form on flag, 4 Line ahead (ships follow in the flagship's wake), 3 Withdraw, and 5 Hold fire (only the flagship fires; AA still defends), which matters at night, when a ship that fires gives herself away.
  - Reserve: a ship held in reserve (Command, Fleet; or a click on the contact panel) stands off and takes no part in the battle.
  - Divisions (owner request): mark your fastest ships and detach them as a fast division to run down an enemy your slowest ship cannot catch. The rest follow as the main body, a fleet of its own while apart (a power at war with you will fight it), and rejoin on signal when they come up.
  - Readiness: each ship's crew and machinery wear with steaming (faster at Full and Flank and in heavy weather, not at Slow) and with battle; a tired ship reloads, steams and fights fires as if short-handed. Lying hove to restores it slowly, days alongside in port quickly (Repairs & crew: the calendar runs on).
- **Nuclear weapons (done, 10 Nov 2026, owner request):** a late research, Nuclear warheads. Your missile submarines carry one warhead to a missile tube, fitted at a port or naval base of your own or by the Enclaves. Fired from the chart at a structure within 600 km, one destroys it for good. Its owner is at war with you from that hour at the lowest standing and never makes peace; its allies lose 60 standing with you, everyone else 30 (the victim's enemies 15); treaties, recognition and any commission are void; and a power of the missile age (or with such an ally) answers in kind against what you hold. The powers never fire first.
- **Playtest feedback, round 12 (done, 10 Nov 2026):**
  - Distance inland is measured to the open sea, not to the nearest lake (Sanpo Farms read 122 km and now reads 1,843); "Set course for the coast" looks for open water a fleet can reach before falling back on the port.
  - The isle south-east of Kotaraya has bays, capes and islets instead of a capsule outline. Structure sites are frozen in `tools/worldgen/sites.lock.json`, so reshaping a coast no longer moves them.
